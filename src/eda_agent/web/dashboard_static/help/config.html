The EDA Agent window and its settings

These settings decide what the AI may do in Altium and how strictly its work is checked. Only you change them: the AI reads them and never switches one itself. Each choice takes effect on the next command, with no restart of Altium or the AI client, and is kept across restarts.

The window

The bridge's window in Altium, a part at a time from the top. The parts above the tabs are the same on every tab.

Permissions

Edits to designs

Whether the AI may change the open schematics, boards and libraries.

Allowed default
The AI may place, move, route, edit and save.
Read-only
Every command that changes a design is refused. Reads, checks, renders and exports still run, so the AI can still review a design.

Use read-only while you review a board you don't want touched, or on a released design. EDA_AGENT_READONLY in the AI client's config also makes it read-only, whatever this says.

Deleting

Whether the AI may delete or remove anything: objects, parts, footprints, nets, sheets, rules, variants, layers, documents from a project, and routing.

Allowed default
Deleting commands run like any other edit.
Refused
Every command that deletes or removes is refused. Other edits still run.

A refused delete can stop a task that has to replace something, such as re-routing a net (its old copper is removed first).

Closing documents

Whether the AI may close a document or a project.

Allowed default
The AI may close what it opened, and what a task needs closed.
Refused
Nothing is closed. Your open documents stay as you left them.

Menus, dialogs, keys

Whether the AI may pick menus, press dialog buttons and type into Altium. The AI always uses Altium's scripting API first. This is the fallback for the few things the API cannot reach.

On default
The AI may drive the interface where there is no API route, and says in its report which step needed it.
Off
No menus, button presses or keystrokes. Some tasks have no other route and are refused.

EDA_AGENT_UI_AUTOMATION=0 in the AI client's config also turns it off, whatever this says.

View

Keep board in view

Whether the window is fitted to the board or sheet after each edit the AI makes.

Off default
The view stays where you put it.
On
After each edit the window is fitted to the board outline, with a margin of a twentieth of its larger side, or to the sheet, at most every 2 seconds. Parts parked off the board do not zoom the view out.

EDA_AGENT_KEEP_BOARD_IN_VIEW=1 in the AI client's config also turns it on.

Lengths in

The unit the AI gives and reads lengths in: coordinates, widths, clearances, thicknesses.

mil default
New AI sessions work in mils.
mm
New AI sessions work in millimetres.

Each session starts in this unit and may switch its own. Every reply says which unit it is in, so a length is never misread.

Values

Component value format

How a component value is written when the AI cleans values up.

None default
Values are left as they are written. Nothing is reformatted.
4K7 2u2
The RKM code of IEC 60062: the letter in place of the decimal point, so a point lost in printing cannot change the value. No unit: 4K7, 2u2, 100n, 3V3, 0R1.
4.7k 2.2u
A decimal point and an SI prefix, without the unit: 4.7k, 2.2u, 100n, 3.3V, 0.1.
with unit
A decimal point and an SI prefix, with the unit: 4.7k, 2.2uF, 100nF, 10uH, 3.3V. Resistors carry no unit, since the ohm sign cannot pass through the bridge.

Dialogs

Tool dialogs

What happens to a dialog a tool opens, such as the Engineering Change Order or the Teardrops options.

Automatic default
The AI answers the dialog as the tool was asked, then reads the result back.
Manual
The dialog waits for you. Use this when you want to read every change order before it is applied.

Checks

Each check runs before a part is built or fab files are written, and refuses the build when what was made does not match its source. All are on by default. A check that is off is skipped, and the result says it was skipped, so the part is never taken for a checked one.

Footprint vs drawing

Checks a footprint's pads against the land pattern in its datasheet drawing.

You might switch it off for lands you changed on purpose: longer for hand soldering, or to a house rule.

Symbol vs pin table

Checks a symbol's pins against the pin table in the part's datasheet.

You might switch it off when the pin table is a picture the AI cannot read, or when you name pins differently on purpose.

Symbol pins vs pads

Checks that the symbol's pin numbers match the footprint's pads, so every pin lands on a pad.

You might switch it off when the footprint lives in an installed library or vault the check cannot read, or when you leave named pads off the symbol on purpose.

3D body vs drawing

Checks a part's 3D body against the outline in its datasheet.

You might switch it off for a deliberately simplified body, or a part whose datasheet has no outline drawing.

SPICE model file

Checks a SPICE model file before it is attached to a part: that the file holds the named model, complete and of the kind it is attached as, with no more nodes than the part has pins, and that its comments name a publisher, as a vendor's model does.

You might switch it off for your own model, measured or written in-house.

Fab export

Checks the board and its parts against the fab's limits before Gerbers, drill files and assembly files are written.

You might switch it off for a limit the fab agreed for this order. For a fab with no data in eda-agent, the AI can use fab "custom" with your own limits instead.

When a check refuses

A refusal is the check doing its job. The AI shows you what differs, in your terms: which land, pin, dimension or node, and by how much. If you still want it built as it is:

  1. Switch that row off here.
  2. Ask the AI to build it again. The result is marked unchecked.
  3. Switch the row back on when you are done.

The AI never passes a check another way, such as placing pads one by one or editing after the build: that would give you the part without the record that it differs from its source.

Diagnostics

Token count

Whether the cost of each AI tool call is counted.

Off default
Tool calls are not counted.
On
Each call's cost in tokens (an estimate from the size of its text and pictures) and its time are counted. The totals show on the dashboard's Tools panel and with eda-agent usage. Nothing is added to what the AI reads.

EDA_AGENT_USAGE_OFF=1 in the AI client's config keeps it off, whatever this says.

Routing

Routing discipline

How strictly the router holds the AI to engineering discipline when it routes. The AI gives the router a routing brief: the layers, widths and impedance of the critical nets, what keeps apart, the loops to keep small, the isolation barriers, each with its source. After each round the router reviews the copper and lists its findings.

Enforced default
A critical net (a differential pair, clock, switching node, high-current path or antenna feed) is left unrouted until the AI gives it a brief entry with its source. A round is written to the board only once the AI has answered every finding of its review, with a reason.
Advisory
Critical nets without a brief are routed and named in the reply. Findings are listed, but the round is written without waiting for answers.
Off
No brief is asked for and nothing waits on findings.

At every level the router obeys the brief the AI did give, and the review still measures the copper. Routes you draw yourself in Altium are never checked or blocked by this setting.

3D models

Basic 3D models

A basic 3D model is a footprint whose 3D bodies are only extruded boxes, cylinders or spheres, with no STEP model of the part among them. It shows roughly where the part is, but not what it looks like.

Not allowed default
When the AI checks footprints or reads a board's parts, it tells you which footprints have a basic model and asks whether to replace them. Each one you agree to is rebuilt as a STEP model from the part's datasheet drawing. A new footprint whose STEP model cannot be built gets no box in its place, and the AI tells you why.
Allowed
Basic models are kept and not reported.

The AI never replaces a model without asking you first. Boxes used as clearance or keep-in volumes are not affected.
